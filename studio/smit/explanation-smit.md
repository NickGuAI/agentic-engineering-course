So basically, this was a hands-on experience with AI agents in action, and how they read studio01.md, README.md, and smit/delegation-card.md. We added some guidelines for them to follow, where they verify the approved sources, check that each item is from an official page, save the 7-day report under outputs/, rerun with a lookback check to produce a comparison report, and trace the change. The included articles' titles, dates, and summaries were checked against the official pages, and any unverified items were left out.
When it comes to human decisions: we chose Claude Code for the run. When the automated fetch of OpenAI's page received a Cloudflare 403, we asked Claude to retry the OpenAI fetch once. After the retry also returned 403, Claude briefly attempted a Wayback Machine fetch. We interrupted that attempt, and its content wasn't used. The assignment involved a changed condition, such as a missing input, a failed check, or a permission boundary; the OpenAI 403 was our failed check, and the 7-to-30-day run was the comparison. We later checked the OpenAI article from the official page in our browser and gave its text to Claude, which verified one September 16 article for the 30-day report.
Along with the setup problem, the uncertainty was whether OpenAI had other relevant news in that date range, since the automated fetch could not get past the Cloudflare 403. The September 16 article falls outside the 7-day window, so it only counts toward the 30-day report. OpenAI's other coverage remains unknown, and the reports correctly say so.
